# FL006 five-sample replacement experiment

2026-10-15. Follow-up to `FL006-BENCHMARK.md`.

## Change and recommendation

Keep the four corner parameter pairs; replace the midpoint with the chord
crossing when available, otherwise the closest chord parameters. Evaluate the
actual curves at that fifth pair. The additive candidate kept both the midpoint
and the chord-derived pair (six samples).

Recommendation: prefer the replacement candidate. It passes the full suite and
produces exactly the same recorded outputs as additive on all 375 benchmark
pairs, with a modest speed advantage in their direct comparison. This does not
prove the midpoint could never help on other inputs. Keep the existing bounded,
heuristic search contract. The five-sample replacement was selected by the user and ported to F#.
Both implementations are selected for adoption; the accompanying commits contain
the ranking change and its regression.

## Benchmarks

Same frozen 375-pair corpus, Node v24.10.0 and Gleam 1.18.1. One warm-up batch per
runtime, followed by seven alternating rounds, sequentially after the test run.
The six-sample generated runtime was copied before editing the source. The
replacement runtime was generated by the successful full validation command.
Runtime/corpus hashes and machine details accompany each JSON record.

| Direct comparison | First median batch | Replacement median batch | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Additive → replacement | 3.331 s | 3.272 s | -1.8% |
| Original → replacement | 3.281 s | 3.339 s | +1.8% |

These are separate paired runs, not a single three-way timing. Their variation
means the 1.8% advantage should not be presented as a guaranteed speedup.

In the original comparison, FL006 alone takes 111.4 ms to miss the crossing
versus 144.4 ms to recover it. The sum of individual medians for the other 374
pairs is 3.156 s original versus 3.128 s replacement (-0.9%); that sum is not the
median of a complete batch. There is no evidence here of a broad slowdown from
replacing the midpoint. The additive comparison's FL006 times are 143.8 ms
versus 147.1 ms, so this particular case did not become faster.

Records:

## Results and remaining caveats

Every first-round output in the additive/replacement comparison is exactly equal:
success/error category, hit count, parameters, and point coordinates. This
includes the nine near-endpoint cases whose hit counts changed relative to the
original solver. Removing the midpoint neither improves nor worsens those
recorded outputs. Those numerical candidate-count questions remain documented
in `FL006-BENCHMARK.md`; matching additive is not an independent proof of their
mathematical correctness.

FL006's full-arc crossing is recovered. The separate tolerance-dependent
short-pair overlap classification affecting default CSG 4295 remains open, as
described in `FL006-INVESTIGATION.md`. This experiment does not close that issue.

## F# port validation

Ported the same four-corner plus chord-derived ranking in
`src/SvgPath/Intersections.fs`, with the matching two-operand-order regression in
`tests/SvgPath.Tests/ElizabethTests.fs` of the sibling F# repository.

Before changing the F# solver, the new regression failed with expected hit count
1 versus actual 0. After the port, `scripts/test-release` passed **2,015 main +
30 slow tests**, including that regression. Logs:

- `remaining-fl006-fsharp-before.log` (expected baseline regression failure)
- `remaining-fl006-fsharp-release.log` (successful full validation)

The .NET runner required permission for its local test communication socket;
the initial sandbox-aborted attempt was rerun with that permission. No F#
performance benchmark or figure regeneration was performed. This ports only the
FL006 ranking change, not other pending Gleam audit fixes or the unresolved CSG
short-pair overlap classification.
